# Research Report: "The Productive Muslim: Where Faith Meets Productivity" by Mohammed Faris

## 1. Author Background and Credibility

### 1.1 Biography
Mohammed Faris (also spelled Muhammad Faris) is the founder of The Productive Muslim Company. According to the official website, he is a Tanzanian young man of Yemeni descent who was pursuing a Master's degree in Finance and Investment at the University of Bristol (UK) in 2007 when he launched ProductiveMuslim.com as a personal blog.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Our Story" page: https://archive.productivemuslim.com/our-story/

### 1.2 Founding of ProductiveMuslim.com
The concept "Productive Muslim" came to Faris on a cold November morning in 2007 when two words "popped into his head." He registered the domain and launched a blog sharing his thoughts on productive living. Two months later he shut it down, believing it was pointless. Six months later, two events reignited his passion: a 10-year-old boy emailed him saying he was a fan, and Faris encountered a hadith that became the catalyst for his mission.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Our Story" page: https://archive.productivemuslim.com/our-story/

### 1.3 The Catalytic Hadith
The hadith that transformed Faris's approach was: "The early hours are blessed for my nation" (Tirmidhi). This connected secular productivity science with Islamic spirituality.

**Sources:**

## 2. The 9 Essential Qualities of the Productive Muslim

*Note: The book's framework organizes productivity around core spiritual and personal qualities derived from Islamic teachings. These qualities form the backbone of the Productive Muslim methodology.*

### 2.1 Sincerity (Ikhlas)
Sincerity is the foundational quality. Every action must be intended for the sake of Allah (Subhanahu Wa Ta'ala). The Productive Muslim motto is: "Have good intentions and work hard!"

The book references the hadith: "Actions are but by intention and every man shall have but that which he intended."

The concept that "a small deed may become great due to the intention behind it, and a great deed may become small due to the intention behind it" is central to the framework.

**Sources:**
- Bukhari and Muslim on the authority of Sahl Ibn Sa'd (May Allah be pleased with him)
- Ibn al-Mubarak, quoted in Siyar a'laam anNubalaa' by Imam ath-Thahabi
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Productivity in Islam - A Sincere Production": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/productivity-in-islam-a-sincere-production/
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Are You Doing Good Deeds with Great Rewards?": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/are-you-doing-good-deeds-with-great-rewards/

### 2.2 Consistency (Al-Muwasarah)
The book emphasizes that the most beloved actions to Allah are those that are consistent, even if small. This is based on the hadith: "The most beloved of deeds to Allah are those that are consistent, even if small."

Consistency is presented as the hardest but most crucial element of the "Productivity Triangle" (Knowledge + Action + Consistency).

**Sources:**
- Bukhari and Muslim on the authority of Aisha (May Allah be pleased with her)
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The Productivity Triangle": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-productivity-triangle/

### 2.3 Patience (Sabr)
Patience is described as one of the key factors to success and personal well-being. The book teaches that patience during hardship maintains productivity and spiritual health.

Allah says in the Quran: "Indeed, Allah is with the patient." (Quran 2:153)

**Sources:**
- Quran 2:153
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Patience and Productivity During Times of Hardship": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/patience-productivity-and-hardship/
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Barakah Academy Reflections: How Do We Develop Patience?": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/barakah-academy-patience/

### 2.4 Trust in Allah (Tawakkul)
The book teaches putting trust in Allah while simultaneously tying one's camel (taking action). This balance between divine reliance and human effort is central.

Allah says: "And whoever puts his trust in Allah, He will be enough for him." (Quran 65:3)

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "If only you relied on Allah a true reliance, He would provide sustenance for you just as He does the birds..."

**Sources:**
- Quran 65:3
- Ahmad, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Al-Hakim, and At-Tirmidhi
- ProductiveMuslim.com "18 Sources of Barakah": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/18-sources-of-barakah/

### 2.5 Excellence (Ihsan)
The concept of Ihsan - doing things beautifully and excellently - is presented as an Islamic principle that should govern all actions.

The Prophet said: "O Allah You have made my creation perfect, so make my moral characteristics also be the best."

**Sources:**
- Bulugh al-Maram
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Increasing Productivity: Lesson 4 - Leading in Excellence": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/increasing-productivity-lesson-4-leading-in-excellence/

### 2.6 Knowledge (Al-Ilm)
Knowledge is the first pillar of the "Productivity Triangle." The Quranic verse "And say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge'" (Quran 20:114) is cited.

The book emphasizes seeking beneficial knowledge through research, asking experts, and making dua.

**Sources:**
- Quran 20:114 (Surah Taha, Verse 114)
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The Productivity Triangle": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-productivity-triangle/

### 2.7 Action (Al-'Amal)
Knowledge without action is worthless. The book emphasizes that action comes from Allah's permission and that starting small (even 10 minutes) can lead to sustained productivity.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The Productivity Triangle": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-productivity-triangle/

### 2.8 Zuhd (Asceticism/Contentment)
The book teaches zuhd not as renouncing the world, but as "holding dunya in your hand, but not letting it into your heart." This is presented as an antidote to consumerism.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "The worldly comforts are not for me. I am like a traveller, who takes a rest under a tree in the shade and then goes on his way."

**Sources:**
- Tirmidhi
- Sahih Bukhari, Book #42, Hadith #7065
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Zuhd: An Anti-dote to Consumerism Culture": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/zuhd-an-anti-dote-to-consumerism-culture/

### 2.9 Community and Social Responsibility
The book emphasizes that productivity is not selfish - the measure of a truly productive person is how they balance responsibilities and add value in family, work, community, and personal/religious development.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "What is Productivity?": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/what-is-productivity/

## 4. Practical Frameworks and Tools

### 4.1 The Productivity Triangle
The core framework consists of three elements:
1. **Knowledge** - Understanding what needs to be done and how
2. **Action** - Taking practical steps
3. **Consistency** - Maintaining effort over time

All three must converge for true productivity.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The Productivity Triangle": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-productivity-triangle/

### 4.2 The Taskinator
The Taskinator is a time-management tool based on the concept of "time blocking." It comes in several forms:

#### Daily Taskinator
- One-sheet solution for daily planning
- Divides the day into four areas: Islam, Family, Work, Personal Development
- Blocks time for each important task
- Emphasizes scheduling most important tasks in early hours
- Available as PDF, fillable PDF, and Excel formats

#### Weekly Taskinator
- Plans the week by determining "Project of the Day" for each day
- Identifies 3 most important tasks per day
- Does not force nitty-gritty daily task planning

#### Ramadan Taskinator
- Specialized version for Ramadan planning
- Bird's-eye view of the month

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The Daily Taskinator": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-daily-taskinator/
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The Weekly Taskinator": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-weekly-taskinator/
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The ProductiveRamadan Ultimate Taskinator": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/ramadan-taskinator/

### 4.3 The Habitator
The Habitator is a habit-tracking tool designed to help develop good habits and destroy bad ones. It tracks three categories:

1. **Spiritual Habits** - e.g., praying Tahajjud, fasting Monday/Thursday, avoiding late for Salah, praying in the Masjid
2. **Physical Habits** - e.g., eating breakfast, jogging, going to the gym
3. **Social Habits** - e.g., visiting orphans/widows, giving da'wah, giving charity, avoiding anger

Scoring system: Each tick = 1 point. Aim for over 20 points per month per habit (ideally 25+).

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "The ProductiveMuslim Habitator": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/the-productivemuslim-habitator/

### 4.4 The Goal Planner
The "Ultimate ProductiveMuslim Goal Planner" is a one-page worksheet for setting achievable goals bi'ithnillah (with Allah's permission).

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Start Here": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/start-here/

### 4.5 Time Management Methodology
Based on Peter Drucker's "Know Thy Time" approach with Islamic modifications:

1. **Analyze your time** - Record time over at least a week
2. **Cut back unproductive demands** - Reduce waste (but not religious obligations!)
3. **Complete tasks in large chunks** - Block 90 minutes of uninterrupted time

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Analyze your time - Your key to productivity": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/analyze-your-time-your-key-to-productivity/
- Peter Drucker, "The Effective Executive"

## 5. The Concept of Barakah

### 5.1 Definition
Barakah is defined as "the attachment of Divine goodness to a thing, so if it occurs in something little, it increases it. And if it occurs in something much it benefits."

Arabic definition provided: "والبركة: هي ثبوت الخير الإلهي في الشيء؛ فإنها إذا حلت في قليل كثرته، وإذا حلت في كثير نفع، ومن أعظم ثمار البركة في الأمور كلها إستعمالها في طاعة الله عز وجل"

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "18 Sources of Barakah": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/18-sources-of-barakah/

### 5.2 The 18 Sources of Barakah
The book identifies 18 sources of barakah:

1. **Good Intentions** - Making deeds for the sake of Allah
2. **Piety and Belief in Allah** - "If the people of the towns had but believed and feared Allah, We should indeed have opened out to them (All kinds of) blessings from heaven and earth..." (Quran 7:96)
3. **Putting your trust in Allah** - Quran 65:3 and the birds parable
4. **Reading Quran** - "And this is a Book which We have sent down, bringing blessings..." (Quran 6:92)
5. **Saying Bismillah** - Invoking Allah's name before any activity
6. **Eating with People** - "Eat together, for blessing is in Jamma'a" (Bukhari)
7. **Honesty in Trade** - Bukhari, Volume 3, Book 34, Number 293
8. **Dua** - Asking Allah for barakah
9. **Halal Income/money** - "O people, Allah is good and He therefore, accepts only that which is good"
10. **Following the Sunnah** - Eating Suhoor, eating with right hand, etc.
11. **Praying Istikhara** - Seeking guidance in all matters
12. **Giving thanks to Allah** - "If ye are grateful, I will add more (favours) unto you" (Quran 14:7)
13. **Charity** - "O son of Adam, spend (in charity), and I'll spend on you!" (Hadith Qudsi)
14. **Tying your ties of kinship** - Bukhari, Volume 6, Book 60, Number 354
15. **Waking up early** - "Allah made the early hours blessed for my Ummah" (Ahmad)
16. **Marriage** - Quran 24:32
17. **Salah** - Quran 20:132
18. **Asking Allah for forgiveness** - "If anyone continually asks forgiveness from Allah, Allah will appoint for him a way out of every distress..."

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "18 Sources of Barakah": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/18-sources-of-barakah/

### 5.3 Barakah as Divine Multiplier
Barakah is described as achieving more with less - the divine multiplier effect. It represents spiritual abundance in time, money, and effort.

The book "The Barakah Effect: More with Less" (published January 2024, 449 pages, Claritas Books, ISBN 978-1-80011-005-2) expands on this concept as "the first practical guide on the concept of Barakah in English."

## 6. Integration of Dunya and Akhirah

### 6.1 The Core Thesis
The book's central argument is that Islam provides a natural framework for productivity. Rather than being a "backward" religion that makes people lazy, Islam gives focus, purpose, and goals - all essential ingredients for productivity.

The Productive Muslim approach rejects the false dichotomy between worldly success and spiritual excellence. Being productive in one's deen naturally leads to productivity in dunya.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "What is Productivity?": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/what-is-productivity/

### 6.2 The Three Pillars
The modern Productive Muslim Company framework organizes around three pillars:

1. **Spiritually Productive** - Mindful worship, intentional dua, daily reflection
2. **Physically Productive** - Sleep, nutrition, movement, energy management
3. **Socially Productive** - Meaningful relationships, community contribution

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com homepage: https://productivemuslim.com

### 6.3 Zuhd: Worldly Engagement Without Attachment
The book teaches that zuhd (often mistranslated as asceticism) means working for dunya but not letting it enter the heart. One should be able to sacrifice worldly possessions for Allah when called upon.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "The worldly comforts are not for me. I am like a traveller, who takes a rest under a tree in the shade and then goes on his way."

**Sources:**
- Tirmidhi
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Zuhd: An Anti-dote to Consumerism Culture": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/zuhd-an-anti-dote-to-consumerism-culture/

### 6.4 Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Approaches
The book presents two approaches to productivity:

**Top-Down:** Starting with a clear vision of one's purpose and what Allah created one for, then achieving mini-goals toward that vision.

**Bottom-Up:** For those without a clear vision - getting involved in interesting projects to discover one's purpose.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "What is Productivity?": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/what-is-productivity/

### 6.5 Daily Adhkar and Remembrance
The book emphasizes incorporating morning/evening remembrances, supplications before sleep, and post-prayer dhikr as productivity boosters that connect daily actions to the hereafter.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "7 Daily Spiritual Productive Habits to Develop": https://archive.productivemuslim.com/7-daily-spiritual-productive-habits-to-develop/

## 7. Key Teachings on Daily Productivity

### 7.1 The Importance of Fajr
The book heavily emphasizes waking up for Fajr as the cornerstone of a productive day. The time after Fajr is described as having immense barakah.

Spiritual tools for waking for Fajr include:
- Knowing who Allah is
- Sincerity in intention
- Wudu before sleep
- Witr prayer + dua
- Reading Quran before sleep
- Remembering the rewards of Fajr salah

The "1.5 hours sleep rule" is also mentioned - aligning sleep cycles to wake refreshed.

## 8. Criticisms and Alternative Perspectives

### 8.1 Limitations of Available Sources
This research is based primarily on:
- The official ProductiveMuslim.com website and its archived content
- The author's official biography and company materials
- The book's store page and related products

**The actual book content could not be directly verified** as the PDF in the project directory could not be parsed, and the book was not available through accessible online previews. The 9 qualities framework is referenced in the book's title and marketing but the exact list and detailed explanations would require direct access to the book.

### 8.2 Potential Criticisms

1. **Commercialization Concerns:** The book and its associated methods have spawned a significant commercial enterprise including paid courses (Tie Your Camel at $XXX, Productive Muslim Certification at $XXX, etc.), which some may view as exploiting religious sentiment for profit.

2. **Individualistic Focus:** Critics might argue that the productivity framework places too much emphasis on individual spiritual improvement without sufficiently addressing systemic issues facing Muslim communities.

3. **Western Self-Help Framework:** Some traditional Islamic scholars may critique the integration of secular productivity science with Islamic spirituality as potentially diluting traditional Islamic practices with modern self-help rhetoric.

4. **Lack of Traditional Scholarship:** The book is written by a businessman and productivity expert rather than a traditional Islamic scholar, which may raise questions about the accuracy of Islamic rulings or interpretations presented.

5. **"Prosperity Gospel" Concerns:** The emphasis on barakah, productivity, and success may inadvertently promote a "prosperity gospel" mindset where material success is seen as a sign of spiritual blessing.

6. **Time Management vs. Trust in Allah:** The balance between human effort (tying the camel) and divine reliance (trust in Allah) can be nuanced and may be misinterpreted as overly emphasizing human control over outcomes.

### 8.3 Alternative Perspectives

1. **Traditional Islamic Perspective:** Traditional Islamic time management emphasizes adherence to the five daily prayers as the primary structuring principle, with productivity as a natural byproduct rather than a goal in itself.

2. **Minimalist Islamic Living:** Some contemporary Muslim thinkers advocate for a more radical rejection of hustle culture, focusing on sufficiency rather than optimized productivity.

3. **Community-Centered Productivity:** Alternative frameworks emphasize collective productivity (building Islamic institutions, community service) over individual self-optimization.

4. **Rest as Worship:** Critics of the "hustle culture" approach argue that rest, contemplation, and even apparent inactivity have value in Islamic tradition.

**Sources:**
- ProductiveMuslim.com "Zuhd: An Anti-dote to Consumerism Culture" (acknowledges these tensions)
- General knowledge of Islamic scholarly discourse on productivity

## 9. Summary and Assessment

"The Productive Muslim: Where Faith Meets Productivity" represents a significant contribution to Islamic self-help literature, bridging secular productivity science with traditional Islamic spirituality. Mohammed Faris's work has:

**Strengths:**
- Made Islamic productivity accessible to a global audience
- Provided practical tools (Taskinator, Habitator) that blend time-management with Islamic values
- Emphasized the centrality of intention (niyyah) in all actions
- Successfully reached over 30,000 people through workshops and online content
- Created a sustainable platform that continues to produce content and programs

**Limitations:**
- The book's framework, while rooted in Islamic principles, is presented in a modern self-help format that may not satisfy traditional scholarly standards
- Heavy commercialization of the brand
- Limited peer-reviewed validation of the specific methodologies
